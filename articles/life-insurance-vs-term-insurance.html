Is There a Difference Between Life Insurance and Term Insurance?

19 October 2026 · 4 min read

"Don't worry, I have life insurance." It is one of the most common sentences in Indian households, and one of the most misleading. Ask which policy, and it is usually an endowment or money-back plan bought years ago, with a sum assured of ₹5 lakh or ₹10 lakh. That is life insurance. It is just not enough of it.

The short answer. Term insurance is life insurance: the pure kind, which pays a large sum if you die during the term and nothing if you don't. "Life insurance" is the family name for every policy that pays on death; most of the other kinds bundle a small cover with a savings plan. The real question is which kind you hold, and whether the cover is big enough.

The family tree

Every life policy sold in India sits on one of these branches. The second column is what your family gets if you are not there.

TypePays on deathPays if you survive the termWhat it is really for
Term planThe full sum assured, say ₹1 croreNothing, unless you paid extra for a "return of premium" versionReplacing your income for your family
Endowment planSum assured plus bonuses declared so farSum assured plus bonuses, as one lump sum at maturityForced saving with a small cover attached
Money-back planSum assured, usually in full, even after earlier payoutsSlices of the sum assured every few years, the rest at maturityForced saving with cash along the way
Whole-life planSum assured, whenever death occurs; cover runs to age 99 or 100A maturity value at a very old age, if at allCover that never runs out; leaving an estate
Guaranteed-return planSum assured, typically 10 times the yearly premiumA fixed income or lump sum, declared on day oneSavings with a known return; cover is incidental
ULIPSum assured or fund value, whichever is higher (most plans)The fund value, after chargesMarket-linked investing in an insurance wrapper

Only the first row pays a large sum for a small premium. The others send most of your premium into a savings pot and keep a thin slice for cover. That is why a ₹50,000-a-year endowment plan often covers you for ₹10 lakh or so, while the same money would buy term cover in the crores.

Why "I have life insurance" so often means under-covered

  • The name. Seller and policy document both say "life insurance". Nobody says "savings plan with a small cover", which is what most of them are.
  • A big premium feels like big cover. But cover is set by the sum assured, not by what you pay for it.
  • "At least something comes back." A term plan returns nothing if you survive, which feels like waste. An endowment "gives it all back with a bonus", but that bonus works out to roughly 4% to 6% a year, and the price of the comfort is the thin cover.

The result: a household that pays a large premium, feels insured, and would get ₹10 lakh if the earning member died. That is two years of a ₹40,000-a-month salary, not a home loan cleared or an income replaced.

What the pure kind actually costs

A healthy 30-year-old non-smoker can buy ₹1 crore of term cover for roughly ₹12,000 to ₹15,000 a year on a 30-year term: about ₹1,000 a month for ten times a typical endowment's cover. Premiums rise with age and smoking, so get live quotes rather than taking these figures on trust. And since 22 September 2025 there is no GST on individual life insurance premiums.

The tax part, in three lines

  • Death benefits are always tax-free, whatever the policy or premium.
  • Maturity money from savings plans (endowment, money-back, guaranteed-return) is tax-free only if your total yearly premium on such policies is ₹5 lakh or less.
  • ULIPs have a lower bar of ₹2.5 lakh a year; above it, maturity gains are taxed.

The three-line rule

  1. Buy a term plan for 10 to 15 times your yearly income plus every loan you owe, running until your dependants no longer need your income.
  2. Save and invest separately, in things built for saving: PPF, mutual funds, deposits. Keep the two jobs apart.
  3. Add up what you already hold. If the total sum assured across every policy in the house is under ten years of income, you are under-covered, however many policies you have.

Already holding an endowment or money-back plan? Do not surrender it in a hurry. The companion piece Death Cover or Survival Benefit? Which Life Insurance Should You Buy puts the two side by side with real numbers.

The bottom line

There is no contest between "life insurance" and "term insurance", because one contains the other. Term insurance is the branch that does the one job insurance exists for: a large payout to your family, for a small premium, if you are not there. The rest of the family tree is mostly savings plans wearing an insurance label. Hold the pure kind for protection, and let your savings grow somewhere built for growing.
